**Iran War:** Tehran's lead negotiator, Mohammad Ghalibaf, stated the "ball is in the U.S. court" for a truce, though Iran maintains its blockade of the Strait of Hormuz until its seven conditions are met. Three tankers caught fire Tuesday after hitting naval mines; October saw a record 37 ship attacks, the highest monthly total of the war.

**Ukraine Talks:** U.S., Russian, and Ukrainian officials held a three-way summit in Abu Dhabi. They agreed to a prisoner exchange, but no breakthrough was reached to end the conflict. Russia continued striking Ukraine’s power grid, hitting six substations, as NATO scrambled jets after four drones strayed into Baltic airspace.

**Ebola Crisis:** The Bundibugyo Ebola strain in Congo has become the second-largest on record, with 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths confirmed. Meanwhile, Kenya moved toward virus-free status by discharging its last patient.

**Climate Summit:** At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Guterres warned that the climate crisis is "outrunning us" after 2025 emissions reached record highs. The U.S. skipped the summit for a second year, leaving Britain's Andy Burnham as the only G7 leader attending.

**Israel:** A new parliament was sworn in after a deadlocked election left Netanyahu without a majority. In Gaza, Israeli strikes killed seven people.

**Spain Strike:** Spain's first general strike in 14 years disrupted transport and led to clashes. Unions organized the protest over housing costs ahead of the 29 November elections.

**Spy Arrest:** A U.S. soldier with a NATO unit was arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia.

**Yemen:** Government strikes on Houthi positions across six provinces continue a conflict that has killed 1,771 people and displaced 300,000 since August.

**U.S. Politics:** The House blocked an impeachment bid against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Scott Colom became the first Black person from Mississippi elected to the Senate in over 140 years.
